Airline Operations Dashboard, 2025

Network performance for a Nigerian domestic carrier: punctuality, demand, delays and route profitability across 8 routes and 6,000 flights.

Sample data generated for demonstration. It models a realistic domestic network, not a real airline’s records. All money is in naira.

What the data says

  • Demand peaks in December. Revenue reached ₦5.57B that month, against an average of about ₦3.65B for January to November.
  • Punctuality drops in the rainy season. On-time performance fell to 58% in July and 55% in August, compared with 75% in January.
  • Evening flights run late. Departures from 18:00 are on time 59% of the time, against 75% for those before 10:00, as delays knock on through the day.
  • Weather is the largest single cause of delays and cancellations, followed by late inbound aircraft.
  • Two routes carry the network. Lagos–Abuja and Lagos–Port Harcourt each earn about a 29% margin, while Lagos–Kano is roughly break-even (−1%) despite a 75% load factor, because of its long distance.

Recommendations

  • Add schedule buffers or spare aircraft for evening departures and for July to September.
  • Review Lagos–Kano pricing or frequency, and test a smaller aircraft on Abuja–Port Harcourt, which earns only a 3% margin.
  • Add capacity in December on the high-load Lagos routes, where demand and fares both rise.
